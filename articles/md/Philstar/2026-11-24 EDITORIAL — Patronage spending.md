# EDITORIAL — Patronage spending

#### The Philippine Star

#### November 23, 2026 | 12:00am

![featured](https://media.philstar.com/photos/2023/05/30/editorialdefault-1_2023-05-30_13-49-27.jpg)

[https://www.philstar.com/opinion/2026/11/23/2571959/editorial-patronage-spending](https://www.philstar.com/opinion/2026/11/23/2571959/editorial-patronage-spending)

The country has an acute shortage of classrooms, school buildings and health facilities. So where does Congress allocate infrastructure funds?

For 2027 alone, the House of Representatives allocated billions for 3,950 multipurpose buildings, according to Sen. Panfilo Lacson. He said these are on top of the 1,584 MPBs that received funding from 2022 to 2026 under the financial assistance subprogram in the Local Government Support Fund.

Some lawmakers have defended the need for MPBs. Since these are tagged as multipurpose, their endorsers should be able to find a need to fit each project. It wouldn’t be too bad if the MPBs are actually needed, and built according to specs instead of being substandard to make room for kickbacks.

Lacson, however, has vowed to present this week proof that certain MPBs are not only ghost or non-existent, but repeatedly funded for three and even four years now in the annual national budget.

Over the past decade, Lacson estimates that a whopping P770 billion has been spent for nearly 79,000 multipurpose buildings.

How many classrooms and health centers could have been built with P770 billion? That amount could have gone to the construction of new roads in Metro Manila so the projects need not have been awarded to the private sector. It would have spared the public from stiff road tolls that add to logistics costs and contribute to inflation.

If the country has those thousands of MPBs, how come evacuees are still housed in school gymnasiums and even classrooms during natural calamities?

Lacson described the MPB allocations as “patronage, a.k.a. ‘pa-pogi’ spending” – something politicians are loathe to forgo in the year leading up to the campaign for the 2028 elections.

Apart from the MPBs, Lacson also flagged at least P241 billion for unconditional cash aid or ayuda programs where politicians have a say.

These include the health pork barrel MAIFIP or Medical Assistance to Indigent and Financially Incapacitated Patients, the multipurpose AICS or Assistance to Individuals in Crisis Situations, TUPAD or Tulong Panghanap-buhay sa Ating Disadvantaged/Displaced Workers and the Tulong Dunong scholarship program.

Like medical and healthcare organizations, Lacson said the budget for MAIFIP should instead be given to the non-politicized Philippine Health Insurance Corp., which is the implementing body for the Universal Health Care Act.

He also wondered why a separate scholarship program that gives politicians endorsement power is needed when public education is free from kindergarten to college.

The Senate resumes deliberations today on the 2027 outlay. If House members are unwilling or unable to learn anything from the unconscionable corruption scandal over public works and the budget process, the Senate should compel the implementation of reforms.
