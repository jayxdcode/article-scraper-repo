# EDITORIAL - The joy of reading

#### November 25, 2026 | 12:00am

There has been some improvement in the most recent participation of the Philippines in the Program for International Student Assessment.

In the 2025 PISA, however, Filipino 15-year-old students still ranked near the bottom globally: 72nd out of 90 countries and territories in reading, 77th out of 91 in science and 74th out of 90 in mathematics.

Still, these are marked improvements since the country began participating in the PISA in 2018, when the Filipino 15-year-olds landed at the bottom in all three competencies.

For the 2025 cycle, the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development, which administers the PISA, cited the Philippines as the fastest-improving participant in reading. While 72nd out of 90 is nothing to crow about, the improvement must be sustained and help pull up performance in science and math.

Reading comprehension is indispensable in grasping concepts in science and math. Interest in reading must be developed and nurtured early in life. This is becoming difficult due to the pervasiveness of digital visual stimuli that compete for children’s attention.

This week’s observance of the 92nd National Book Week focuses not only on developing foundational reading skills among young children, but also on restoring and strengthening interest in traditional reading across all ages.

The week’s theme – basa, salin, daloy, or read, pass on and flow – emphasizes active reading, sharing stories and translating ideas, and keeping flowing within the community what has been learned from reading.

The special week is being observed alongside the 36th observance of Library and Information Services Month. Many young Filipinos may no longer be familiar with libraries, and the government must step in to address this deficiency.

Local governments must work with national government agencies, the academe, parents and civil society organizations in reviving interest in books and helping people discover the joy of reading.

The private sector is doing its part, but the government must take the lead in promoting literacy and interest in traditional reading.

Funding is needed for public libraries and quality books, with sufficient support for publishers, authors and book translators. The efforts must reach even geographically isolated areas where literacy is low.

Literacy and reading comprehension are keys to innovation and competitiveness that are essential to national progress. They are needed for navigating the mountain of disinformation and malicious content, and distinguishing truth from fake news in the age of artificial intelligence. They are critical for the nation to survive and excel in a globalized environment.
